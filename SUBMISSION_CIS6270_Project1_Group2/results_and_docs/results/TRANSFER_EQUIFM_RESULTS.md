## What this run is

Our seven inference-time guidance arms, run on a **borrowed** flow-matching
generator — EquiFM (Song et al., NeurIPS 2023), released EMA weights, md5
`89db17e7f793` — instead of our own base model, to ask whether the guidance
survives on someone else's generator. Every arm is scored through **TFG's
property pair**: the guide is TFG `tf_predict_<p>` at time 0, the oracle f_B is
EDM's classifier (TFG `evaluate_<p>`), and a **second oracle** (OC-Flow's clean
EGNN, different weights) is reported beside it in every cell. The target is
`dist` at **q90**, fixed per property. The band half-width is
δ = 2 × MAE(f_B), so in-band is defined *by this pair* and by nothing else.
Three stages ran on Betty over 23–24 Sep 2026: a strength screen (`compare`
+ `extend`, n = 512), the headline at the frozen strengths (`full`, n = 5000),
and the tune half of an equal-chemistry study (`eqtune`, n = 2000).

## 7. Script changes made

**`proj1/scripts/dist_report.py` — one defect fixed.** Run as the runbook
gives it, it refused:

```
mu: cells were scored with delta [0.15675363622791122, ...] but the equifm ladder uses 0.251947 -- wrong --backend?
```

`--backend` was **not** wrong. `_tfg_pair()` calls
`transfer_sweep.build_pair(...)` positionally and never passed `delta_mode`.
`build_pair` gained that parameter later (for the base-model comparison) with
default **`"local"`**, while every `tfg` and `equifm` cell on disk was written
under the **global** rule — and the `--backend ours` branch of `build_ladder`
also uses the global rule. So the ladder was silently built on a δ about 1.6×
the cells', and the delta gate then refused it. The gate did its job: **no
wrong number was ever published from this**, and the already-documented
EDMsecond transfer ([FULL_RUN_RESULTS.md](FULL_RUN_RESULTS.md) §4) would have
hit the same refusal on a re-run.

Changes, all backward-compatible:
- added module constant `DELTA_MODE_DEFAULT = "global"`;
- `_tfg_pair(...)` takes `delta_mode` and passes it explicitly, with a comment
  recording why the default must not be inherited from `build_pair`;
- `build_ladder(...)` takes and forwards `delta_mode`;
- new CLI flag `--delta-mode {global,local}`, default `global`.

`--backend ours` is untouched (it never reached `_tfg_pair`), and the default
reproduces what every cell on disk was scored with.

**`proj1/scripts/transfer_equifm_table.py` — new.** The two scripts the runbook
names do not print four things this write-up needs, so they are here:
atom_stability and the two uniqueness columns; the second oracle's in-band;
a head-to-head of *every* guided arm against unguided **and** plug with explicit
differences and z (independent and paired, continuous and decoded); and cost
per stage. It also prints the compare-stage screen with recomputed edge/floor
status, and eqtune coverage. It **never re-decides FR5** — `full_run_table.py`
remains the pre-registered verdict, and where the two overlap they agree
number-for-number (checked on the `btvg`-vs-`plug` rows of all three
properties). Paired columns are computed only after `mol_idx` **and** `n_atoms`
are verified identical between the two sidecars; otherwise it prints `n/a`.

No file under `results/` or `logs/` was modified.

## What each outcome shows, in plain words

1. **The guidance does transfer onto a borrowed generator — the machinery
   works.** All seven arms ran end to end on EquiFM, wrote finite cells with no
   failures, and the screen picked a sensible strength for each. `tfg` was
   ported and frozen before any cell ran, so on this generator no arm is post
   hoc. That is the question the transfer was built to ask, and the answer is
   yes.

2. **But almost nothing beats the trivial baseline.** On alpha and gap, no arm
   reaches the #Atoms rung — predicting the property from atom count alone is
   as good. Only `lgd_mc` and `tfg`, and only on mu, clear it. The honest
   summary is: on someone else's generator, at a 0.9× chemistry budget, most of
   these methods buy very little.

3. **`lgd_mc` is the one arm that clearly does something**, on mu (in_band
   0.120 vs unguided 0.075, z_ind +7.56; decoded +6.46) and on gap (0.068 vs
   0.054, z_ind +3.09; decoded +4.29). It also beats `plug` on mu at z_ind
   +6.71. This is the same arm that wins on our own base model, so the ranking
   survives the change of generator on mu — one seed, so not replicated.

4. **`tfg` gains on mu, but much of it is in the soft features.** Continuous
   in_band +0.0296 (z_ind +5.17) drops to +0.0148 decoded (z_ind +2.72), and
   its MAE/δ goes 6.377 → 7.751 when decoded, the largest soft-to-decoded gap
   of any arm. It also pays the most chemistry: mol_stab 0.7754 and validity
   0.869, both the lowest in the table. Report `tfg` decoded or not at all.

5. **`btvg` does not beat `plug` anywhere.** Its largest in-band edge over plug
   is gap +0.0062 (z_ind +1.28), and against unguided its best is gap +0.0122
   (z_ind +2.58, below the bar; decoded z_ind +3.90 is above it, and the
   supplementary Holm-adjusted paired sign test gives p 0.00438 — all on one
   seed, so this is the one place `btvg` shows anything, and it is not a
   result). The
   pre-registered FR5 table returns "tie" on every comparison except mu, where
   `lgd_mc` and `tfg` **beat** `btvg`. On the screen, FR1 recorded `btvg` beaten
   on 3 of 3 properties at both targets. This is consistent with what
   [the arm-ranking rubric](ARM_RANKINGS.md) and the main run already say, now
   on a second generator.

6. **`btvg_var` is a null on this generator too.** In-band differences from
   unguided are +0.0002 to −0.0002 on mu and alpha and +0.0022 on gap, none
   close to significant; its MAE is essentially unguided's. The variance term
   again fails to raise in-band.

7. **The chemistry floor is what sets every strength.** 15 of 18 screen picks
   are floor-limited: left unconstrained, every one of those arms would run at
   a higher strength. Any statement like "arm X gains Y" is a statement about
   what X can buy *inside a 0.9× stability budget*, and the budget, not the
   method, is doing much of the work.

8. **The second oracle agrees with the first.** OC-Flow's in-band tracks f_B's
   across all 21 cells (e.g. mu `lgd_mc` 0.1124 vs 0.1202, gap `lgd_mc` 0.0714
   vs 0.0684), and never reverses an arm ordering. The gains that exist are not
   an artefact of one evaluator.

9. **The equal-chemistry comparison — the thing chain 2 was for — has no
   result.** It was never frozen, and gap never started. Nothing in this
   document is an equal-chemistry number.

## Caveats

1. **This is a q90 run; the paper reports q50 only.** By the 26 Sep scope
   decision, **no number on this page goes into the paper or the slides.** It is
   supporting material for the team. The q50 cells exist only at the screen
   stage (165 of the 342 compare cells); no q50 cell was ever run at n = 5000.

2. **In-band here is not comparable to any run scored with a different property
   pair.** δ = 2 × MAE(f_B) sets the band width, and f_B here is TFG's
   `evaluate_<p>`: δ = 0.15675 / 0.16480 / 0.0037361. Our own pair gives
   different δ (e.g. mu 0.16799 in full run v2), so an in-band fraction from
   this page may **not** be placed beside one from `results/full`,
   `results/v3` or `results/basecmp`. Compare MAE in published units, or
   compare within this page only.

3. **One seed.** Every table in §3 and §5 rests on seed 20261001. Seed-to-seed
   spread is unmeasured (every `seed sd` is `nan`), and this project has
   already been bitten by single-seed effects. Treat every z here as
   provisional; the project's own bar is replication across seeds.

4. **What is post hoc.** The §4 screen statuses (`floor_limited`, `grid_edge`,
   `on_extension`) are **recomputed** here — `frozen_q90.json` carries no such
   keys, and no `collapse_contaminated` status is asserted from it. The §6
   `eqtune` mu frontier is post hoc: `eqfreeze` never ran. The decision to
   report seed 20261001 alone is also post hoc, forced by what survived. What
   is **not** post hoc: the arm set, the grid, the edge rule, the FR3a rule and
   `tfg`'s inclusion were all fixed in plan §11 before any EquiFM cell existed,
   and `frozen_q90.json` records `tfg_post_hoc: false`.

5. **The coordinate covariance is an approximation.** EquiFM aligns its
   coordinate noise to the data (Kabsch + Hungarian), so the covariance
   identity our arms assume does not hold exactly. `plug` uses no covariance
   and is exact; `tmpd`, `lgd_mc`, `btvg` and `btvg_var` all inherit the same
   coordinate-block surrogate (plan §10.3). Differences among those four are
   differences *given* that shared approximation.

6. **The generator saw both halves of QM9.** Guide and oracle are disjoint and
   measured to be so, but EquiFM trained on both halves, so the oracle is not
   fully held out from the generator. Accepted and disclosed by the 23 Sep
   decision; every arm stands on the same generator, so it does not favour one
   arm over another. δ is optimistic for the same reason.

7. **The unguided row is not EquiFM's published row.** We run EquiFM's own
   Euler grid at 100 steps; the paper used dopri5. Its mol_stability here is
   0.8588 at n = 5000.

8. **The chemistry floor at freeze was measured at n = 512** (0.7910 = 0.9 ×
   0.8789) but the full-scale unguided stability is 0.8588, giving a full-scale
   floor of 0.7729. Both are reported above; the strengths were chosen under
   the first.

9. **Equal `w` is not equal force**, and the frozen strengths span 0.01 to 16.
   No row here is "method X at the same setting as method Y"; each is "method X
   at its own best floored setting". Any strength-confound caveat that travels
   with the main run travels with this one.

10. **`full_run_table.py`'s `ARMS` list and its `OURS = "btvg"` family are the
    main run's**, so its FR5 table is `btvg` against everything. That is the
    pre-registered family and it is reported as such in §5; the
    every-arm-vs-unguided-and-plug family in §5 is **supplementary** and was
    not pre-registered for this run.
