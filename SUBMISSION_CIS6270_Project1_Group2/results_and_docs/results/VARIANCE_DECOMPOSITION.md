# Is `V_F` the variance BTVG needed to reduce? A direct measurement

Generated by `proj1/scripts/variance_decomp_table.py` from `proj1/scripts/variance_decomposition.py`. Do not hand-edit.

**The question.** BTVG's variance term descends `V_F = g' Sigma g`, the local predictive variance of ONE trajectory's property under the interpolant posterior. The metric it is meant to improve is the ACROSS-MOLECULE spread of terminal residuals. Those are different quantities. This measures both, on real trajectories, at every guided step:

| symbol | what it is | who acts on it |
|---|---|---|
| `within(t) = mean_i V_F,i(t)` | the interpolant posterior's variance, averaged over molecules | **BTVG's objective** |
| `across(t) = Var_i[f(m_i(t))]` | the dispersion of where trajectories are heading | nothing in BTVG |
| `terminal = Var_i[f_B(x_1^i)]` | the spread the band is scored on | the metric |

For a deterministic sampler the continuation from `x_t` is a single point, so there is no realised within-trajectory variance: `V_F` is the spread of a posterior the ODE never draws from, and it goes to zero on its own as `k = (1-t)^2/t` falls. The terminal spread is the limit of `across`. Everything below is in units of delta^2.

**Provenance.** n = 256, seed 20261001, q90 target, 100-step euler, guidance window t >= 0.5. The unguided rows are logged by running `btvg_var` at w = 0, which computes the field and multiplies it by zero; the script verifies the resulting path is bit-identical to a real unguided run (max |d f_B| = 0) and aborts otherwise.

## What this supports

1. **BTVG's lever vanishes on its own.** In every run `within` ends below 0.2% of the total (alpha/unguided 0.0%, mu/btvg 0.2%, mu/unguided 0.1%). It is not driven to zero by guidance; `k = (1-t)^2/t` takes it there regardless of what any arm does.
2. **The terminal spread is carried entirely by `across`.** It settles within 5% of the terminal variance from t = 0.64 (alpha/unguided), t = 0.95 (mu/btvg), t = 0.76 (mu/unguided). `across` dips mid-window and re-widens toward the end in **every** run including the unguided one, so that re-widening is the flow's own behaviour and must not be attributed to guidance. What matters for BTVG is that this is the term carrying the terminal spread, and no part of its objective touches it.
3. **`V_F` does not identify the molecules that miss.** Across every run and every late step, the correlation is **-0.174 to +0.119** -- indistinguishable from none, and mostly the wrong sign. Reducing `V_F` therefore cannot preferentially rescue the molecules that were going to land outside the band.
4. **This is not an artefact of looking at unguided states.** The same pattern holds on `btvg`-guided trajectories at its own strength.

**Not supported.** This measures the relation between `V_F` and the terminal spread; it does not prove that no variance-based objective can work, only that this one targets a quantity which is a fraction of a percent of the terminal spread by the end of sampling and is uninformative about which molecules miss. Properties other than those listed were not run. One seed each, and the correlations are over the molecules that had a positive finite V_F at that step, which is fewer than n.
