Part 3 · Test transfer and close the story

Conclusion and future directions

Back to the hypothesis. Does making batch dispersion a requested quantity raise band coverage? At the power we have, no — no rung beats plug-in at a corrected significance level. But we can say why, not just that.

Strongest supported claim

Servoing the deviation gain against a batch-dispersion setpoint gives monotone, two-sided, modality-portable control of the generated batch's spread at no additional sampling cost. It converts the coverage-against-fidelity trade-off into a quantity the user requests — rather than removing it.

Mechanism and trade-off

The coefficient is affine in Fᵢ, so BDG cannot leave the plug-in field's reachable set. It changes which point of that set the sampler lands on, and how it gets there: holding centring at w while servoing only the deviation gain buys contraction from the deviation mode instead of scaling the whole field — which is why chemistry survives at matched coverage.
