Part 3 · Test transfer and close the story

Conclusion and future directions

Back to the hypothesis. We asked whether making batch dispersion a requested quantity raises band coverage. At the power we have, it does not: no rung beats the plug-in rule at a corrected significance level. And we can say why, rather than only that.

The strongest supported conclusion

Servoing the deviation gain of a property-gradient guidance term against a batch-dispersion setpoint gives monotone, two-sided, modality-portable control of the generated batch's spread at no additional sampling cost. It converts the coverage-against-fidelity trade-off into a quantity the user requests, rather than removing it.

Mechanism and trade-off

The per-sample coefficient is affine in Fᵢ, so BDG is the plug-in field at a rescaled weight aiming at a shifted target: it cannot leave that field's reachable set. It changes which point of that set the sampler lands on, and how. Holding the centring gain at w while servoing only the deviation gain buys contraction from the deviation mode, not by scaling the whole field, which is why BDG retains more chemistry at matched coverage.

Specific next experiments

Signed-w plug-in control, with our pre-registered drop rule: if it reaches BDG's spread at equal chemistry, we withdraw the controller claim and report the decomposition instead.

Matched clip activity, to separate clip-limited from controller-limited rungs.

A second seed pair for the open-loop replay.

A richer M2 property (cell-type classifier logit, nonlinear and motif-driven), to test whether the magnitude gap is the property map.
